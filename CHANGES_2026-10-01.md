## 2. Create Subscription always failed (but still created the subscription)

**Cause:** after `create_subscription` had already created the row, the
server checked the route against a table that does not exist
(`delivery_area_pincodes`; the real one is `pincodes`). Every attempt showed
"Route does not serve the address pincode area", and the route, address and
payment method were never saved on the new subscription. Retrying created
another one.

**Fix (`services/subscriptionService.ts`):** everything is checked **before**
anything is created — address belongs to the customer and isn't deleted,
pincode is served, route (if chosen) belongs to that area, dates, label, items.
A refusal leaves nothing behind.

**Form now:**
- several products, each with − / + quantity (1–99, up to 10 products);
- optional label (≤ 30) and end date;
- wallet / cash-on-delivery choice;
- per-delivery cost, and a 3-day wallet hint;
- **route is optional** — app subscriptions never have one, so it is no longer
  required for staff-created ones either.

**Start date bug:** the default was the UTC date, so between midnight and
5:30 AM it showed **yesterday** (the screenshot said 30-09 at 12:42 AM on
1 Oct). It now uses India time and can't be set in the past (form and server).

## 5. Security: taking over an ad-hoc customer's wallet (migration 032)

**Problem:** `claim_adhoc_user` runs after every app sign-in and merges a
matching ad-hoc record into the new account. Migration 009 meant to match only
the identity in the login token, but it fell back to the **phone sent by the
app** when the token had none — true for every email sign-up. Anyone could
sign up and send an ad-hoc customer's phone number to receive that customer's
wallet, addresses and orders.

**Fix — `docs/db/migrations/032_claim_adhoc_user_hardening.sql`:**
- App sign-ins match **only** the token's email and phone (phone compared as
  10 digits, so `919876543210` = `9876543210`). Values in the request are never
  used for matching.
- If the email and phone point at different ad-hoc records, the **email**
  (verified at sign-up) wins. A genuine tie is refused, not guessed.
- The admin panel's Convert passes the record's id (`p_adhoc_id`, service role
  only), so exactly that record is converted; it is refused if the email or
  phone belongs to another customer.
- New helper `internal.normalize_phone_10`. App calls are unchanged (the new
  parameter is optional).

**Decision recorded:** automatic merge on sign-up is **kept** for the verified
email / phone — it is how a staff-created customer gets their history when they
install the app. The risk of a staff typo in the email is reduced by the new
warning on the form (§3).

**Tests:** `docs/db/tests` — 13 new (spoofed phone refused, email / phone
claims, `+91` matching, email-over-phone precedence, ties, `p_adhoc_id` rules,
anon blocked, collisions). **106 passed, 0 failed** (93 → 106).
Snapshots: `functions.sql`, `functions_internal.sql`, `DATA_MODEL.md`.

## 6. Route per address, delivery dates, and one merged Deliveries screen (migration 033)

Groundwork for adopting the operating model the client's current panel uses:
one delivery run per route per day, carrying subscription items and one-time
orders together. **Added alongside the existing screens** — Daily Ops, Orders
and Subscriptions are untouched, so both models can be compared before the old
ones are retired.

**Decisions taken (client's model):**

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Where the route lives | On the **address**, assigned by staff. Everything delivered there uses it. |
| Initial route | **None** — every address starts Unassigned. Existing subscriptions' routes are backfilled onto their address, so nothing that works today changes. |
| Route scope | A route belongs to one delivery area; an address can only be given a route of the area that serves its pincode. |
| One-time order delivery date | **Chosen by the system**: the area's next delivery day, moved one day later if the order is placed after the area's cut-off. |
| Delivery frequency | Stays **per area** (1 = daily, 2 = alternate days). Not moved onto subscriptions. |
| Short delivery of a prepaid order | The undelivered value is **refunded to the wallet**; the order's own totals never change. |

**Database (`033_route_per_address_and_delivery_dates.sql`):**
- `addresses.route_id`, set only through `set_address_route` (staff only). A
  trigger refuses a route from the wrong area and blocks customers from setting
  it at all. If a customer edits their address into another area the route
  clears, so the address simply becomes Unassigned and the app keeps working.
- `create_subscription` now stores `address_id` (app subscriptions left it
  empty, so there was no way to find their address or route), with a backfill
  for existing rows and a foreign key so the panel can read the address in one
  query.
- One-time orders get `expected_delivery_date` on insert, from the area's
  schedule and cut-off. Done in a trigger, so the tested `place_order` money
  path is untouched. Open orders without a date are backfilled.
- `order_items.delivered_qty`, `orders.delivered_at`, `orders.tracking_info`.
- `record_order_delivery(order, items, note)` — staff record what was actually
  delivered. Every line must be accounted for. Nothing delivered marks the
  order cancelled; a shortfall on a **paid** order credits the difference to
  the wallet (`order_refund`). Order totals are never rewritten, so the
  migration 012 money invariants still hold.
- `reschedule_order(order, date)` — move an order to another day.
- `internal.next_delivery_date(area, from)` mirrors the app/run-sheet rule, and
  new `internal.today_ist()` because Postgres runs in UTC and `CURRENT_DATE` is
  still yesterday until 5:30 AM IST.

**Admin panel — two new pages:**
- **Customer Addresses** (`/admin/addresses`): every address with a route
  dropdown, All / Assigned / Unassigned filters, an unassigned count, and area
  shown per row. The dropdown offers only that area's routes and is disabled
  with an explanation when the pincode is in no delivery area. This is the only
  place a route is set.
- **Deliveries** (`/admin/deliveries`): pick a date and route and get one card
  per address — the subscription items due and the one-time orders for that
  day, side by side. Delivered quantity per item, a note, Save, Reschedule for
  one-time orders, a refund line when one is made, already-delivered rows
  collapsed, a printable route sheet, and Unassigned addresses in their own
  group with a link to fix them.

## 6b. Deliveries screen — fixes from first testing (migration 034)

Found while testing §6 against live data:

1. **Print was blank.** The print CSS hid everything and re-showed only a tiny
   header, never the stop cards. Rebuilt around a `#delivery-print-area`
   wrapper (header + stops) that is the only thing visible when printing; each
   stop is kept together on a page.
2. **Reschedule ignored the area's schedule.** Picking 3 Oct for a Noida order
   left it on 3 Oct, a day Noida doesn't deliver. **Migration 034** makes
   `reschedule_order` snap the chosen date **forward to the area's next
   delivery day** (3 Oct → 4 Oct for Noida); an all-India order with no area
   keeps the exact date. The picker defaults to the order's own delivery date
   and the dialog says it may move forward.
3. **Cancelled orders showed up.** Now always excluded. "Show delivered" is
   relabelled **"Include delivered"** with a tooltip; delivered orders still
   appear only on their exact date.
4. **Overdue orders.** One-time orders past due and still undelivered now
   **roll forward** into the selected date's list (matching the client's
   panel), with a red count banner and an **"Overdue · due &lt;date&gt;"** badge
   on each. Subscriptions stay exact-date.

An order whose pincode maps to **no active area** (e.g. 500032 / Hyderabad in
the test data) correctly gets no delivery date and never appears here.
