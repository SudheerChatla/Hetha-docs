# 2026-07-28 — Price / delivery-charge tampering audit and fixes

Scope: every path where money is decided. Everything below was reachable with
nothing more than the app's published anon key plus a normal customer login —
no admin credentials, no leaked secrets.

Migrations added: `docs/db/migrations/007_money_integrity.sql`,
`008_payment_intents.sql`, `009_privilege_lockdown.sql`,
`010_grant_hardening.sql`, `011_legacy_function_grants.sql`.
Edge function rewritten: `Hetha_app/supabase/functions/razorpay/index.ts`.
Client updated: checkout, wallet, delivery-charge and razorpay services.

---

## 1. What was exploitable

### Delivery charge (the direct answer to "can it be tampered with?" — yes)
`DeliveryChargeService.calculateDeliveryCharge()` computed the fee **in the
Flutter client** and the value was passed to `place_order(p_delivery_charge …)`,
which stored it without checking.

- `p_delivery_charge = 0` → free delivery on any order.
- `p_delivery_charge = -100000` → order total below the goods value. With
  `payment_method` = `cod`/`razorpay` (no ledger row to trip the
  `wallet_transactions.amount > 0` check) this produced a valid, "placed" order
  worth less than nothing.
- The same value was passed again, unchecked, through the edge function's
  `verify-order-payment` action.

### Item prices
- `place_order` did re-read variant prices (good), but never validated
  `quantity`: a negative or fractional quantity flowed straight into the
  subtotal arithmetic.
- `create_subscription` took **`unit_price` from the client payload**. The daily
  run sheet bills off `subscription_items.unit_price`
  (`Hetha_admin/services/dailyOps/generateRunSheet.ts` sums
  `unit_price * quantity`), so one tampered subscription meant permanently
  discounted daily deliveries.
- `subscription_items` RLS allowed the subscription owner to INSERT/UPDATE rows
  directly, so the price could also be rewritten without any RPC.

### Wallet
- `users.wallet_balance` was directly writable over PostgREST:
  `from('users').update({wallet_balance: 999999}).eq('id', myId)` — RLS allows
  own-row UPDATE and there were no column-level grants.
- `update_wallet_balance(...)` is `SECURITY DEFINER`; Postgres grants EXECUTE to
  PUBLIC by default, so any logged-in user could call it with
  `p_type => 'credit'` and any amount.
- `finalize_daily_run(...)` was likewise PUBLIC-executable: a customer could
  finalize the whole day's run and trigger every customer's wallet deduction.

### Razorpay
- `create-order` used the **client's** amount.
- `verify-payment` (wallet top-up) credited **`body.amount`** after only
  checking the HMAC. The signature proves `<order_id>|<payment_id>` is genuine;
  it says nothing about how much was paid. Pay ₹1, post `amount: 100000`, get
  ₹1,00,000 of credit — and the same triple could be replayed for repeat
  credits.
- `verify-order-payment` never compared the amount paid to the order value, and
  replaying one successful payment placed unlimited "paid" orders.

### Adjacent authorization gaps found on the way
- `place_order` / `create_subscription` never checked `p_user_id` against
  `auth.uid()` despite being `SECURITY DEFINER`.
- `orders` INSERT was allowed for `user_id = auth.uid()` → hand-written orders
  with `total = 0, payment_status = 'paid'`.
- `upsert_pauses` / `remove_paused_dates` took only a subscription id, with no
  ownership check.
- `claim_adhoc_user` matched ad-hoc accounts on **client-supplied** email/phone,
  so a caller could claim someone else's ad-hoc row and its wallet balance.

### Client-side-only validation that existed before (all now also server-side)
| Rule | Was | Now |
|------|-----|-----|
| Delivery-charge tiers | `delivery_charge_service.dart` | `internal.compute_delivery_charge()` |
| 3-day subscription wallet buffer | `checkout.dart`, `create_subscription_page.dart` | `place_order`, `create_subscription` |
| Wallet sufficiency | UI disabled the button | `place_order` (buffer-aware) |
| Serviceable pincode / delivery frequency | `delivery_area_service.dart` | still client-side (see residual risks) |
| Max 5 subscriptions | — | already in the RPC |

## 2. What changed

**007 — server-authoritative pricing**
- `internal` schema (not exposed through PostgREST) holds the privileged
  primitives: `apply_wallet_delta`, `normalize_cart`, `compute_delivery_charge`,
  `place_order_core`, `create_subscription_core`, plus caller-identity helpers
  (`jwt_role`, `is_service_actor`, `is_admin_actor`).
- `normalize_cart` is the single parser for cart payloads: uuid format check,
  whole-number quantity 1–99, duplicate merge, and a join that rejects unknown,
  inactive or out-of-stock variants. Prices/labels/weights come from the catalog.
- `place_order` now: checks the caller, whitelists the payment method, prices the
  cart itself, computes the delivery charge itself (`p_delivery_charge` honoured
  only for admins), enforces the 3-day buffer, and parks customer-initiated
  `razorpay` orders in `payment_pending` until payment is verified.
- `create_subscription` (both overloads) ignores client prices and enforces the
  buffer.
- `quote_cart(p_cart_items)` is the read-only quote the client displays.
- `update_wallet_balance` is now an authorization wrapper; the 5-arg overload was
  dropped (it made 5-argument calls ambiguous — "function is not unique").

**008 — payment intents**
- `payment_intents` table (service-role only) records the amount the *server*
  decided before checkout opens.
- `finalize_wallet_topup` / `finalize_order_payment` are idempotent per
  `razorpay_payment_id`, refuse underpayment, and cap credit at the intent
  amount. Partial UNIQUE index on `orders.razorpay_payment_id`.

**009 — privilege lockdown**
- `REVOKE UPDATE ON users` + per-column re-grant that excludes
  `wallet_balance`.
- `subscription_items` and `orders` writes are admin-only (customers read only);
  the `SECURITY DEFINER` RPCs still write as the table owner.
- Trigger blocks non-admins from changing `subscriptions.user_id` /
  `payment_method`.
- `finalize_daily_run` gated on `daily_ops:edit`; it now calls
  `internal.apply_wallet_delta` so a daily-ops admin doesn't need
  `customers:edit`.
- Ownership checks added to `upsert_pauses` / `remove_paused_dates`.
- `claim_adhoc_user` matches on the JWT's verified email/phone.
- Implicit PUBLIC EXECUTE grants dropped from every money RPC.

**010 — grant hardening (found while verifying the live deployment)**
- Supabase configures `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES IN SCHEMA public GRANT EXECUTE
  ON FUNCTIONS TO anon, authenticated, service_role`, so each new function gets
  an **explicit** grant to those roles. 009's `REVOKE ... FROM PUBLIC` does not
  remove those, which left `anon` with EXECUTE on `place_order` and
  `authenticated` with EXECUTE on the payment-intent functions after deployment.
- Neither was exploitable (the in-function guards reject both callers), but 010
  revokes the grants explicitly so the privilege surface matches the intent, and
  the test harness now models Supabase's default privileges so this class of
  mistake fails the suite.

**011 — legacy function grants (found by auditing the live grant list)**
- Functions that 007–010 never re-created still carried the implicit
  `GRANT EXECUTE TO PUBLIC` from their original `CREATE FUNCTION`, and
  `has_function_privilege('anon', …)` is true whenever PUBLIC holds it — so
  revoking from `anon` alone had no effect. Live check after 010 showed `anon`
  able to execute `cancel_subscription`, `get_user_daily_commitment`,
  `update_address_as_default`, `get_user_role`, `rls_auto_enable` and
  `set_reviews_updated_at`.
- `update_address_as_default` was the one that mattered: `SECURITY DEFINER` and
  trusting `p_user_id`, so a caller with a victim's user id + address id could
  rewrite that address. It also happened to be broken (wrote
  `addresses.phone` / `addresses.updated_at`, neither of which exists — one of
  the pre-existing latent bugs). Rewritten with the correct columns
  (`phone_number`, accepts either `phone` or `phone_number` in the payload,
  no `updated_at`) plus an ownership check, and it now raises when the address
  doesn't belong to the user instead of silently doing nothing.
- `has_permission()` / `is_super_admin()` deliberately keep their `anon` grants:
  RLS policies reference them and policy expressions run as the calling role.
- End state: `anon` can execute only `quote_cart`, `has_permission`,
  `is_super_admin` — asserted by the harness.

**012 — money invariants enforced by the database**
- 007–011 made the RPCs authoritative, but staff holding `orders:edit` /
  `subscriptions:edit` can still write those tables directly (the admin panel
  needs it for status changes and run-sheet edits). So a future admin feature
  doing a plain INSERT would have bypassed the server pricing. The arithmetic is
  now a database rule rather than a convention:
  - `orders`: `subtotal = Σ order_items.total_price`,
    `total = subtotal + delivery_charge`, `delivery_charge >= 0`, at least one
    line item. DEFERRED constraint triggers on both `orders` and `order_items`,
    so they run at COMMIT and don't care who did the writing.
  - `order_items`: `unit_price` snapped to the catalog on INSERT, `total_price`
    always derived. Historical rows keep their snapshot on UPDATE.
  - `subscription_items`: `unit_price` snapped to the catalog on INSERT and
    **immutable** afterwards — this is the column the daily run sheet bills
    against, so it is the highest-value target in the schema. It also means the
    admin panel's `addSubscriptionItem` can no longer set a price, whatever it
    sends.
  - `subscription_daily_orders`: `total_value = Σ` its items.
  - Escape hatch for deliberate repair: `SET LOCAL hetha.skip_money_checks =
    'on'` — a session GUC, so it is not reachable through PostgREST.
- Fixed a real drift bug the invariant exposed:
  `Hetha_admin/services/dailyOps/orders.ts:updateDailyOrderItem` changed an
  item's `total_price` without re-summing the parent
  `subscription_daily_orders.total_value` — and `finalize_daily_run` debits the
  wallet from that column, so an ops quantity edit silently billed the old
  amount. It now re-sums the parent (and validates the quantity).
- Stale comments in `Hetha_admin/services/orderService.ts` corrected
  (`HET-` → `ORD-<timestamp>-<nnnn>`, `initiated_by`), plus an explicit warning
  not to replace the RPC call with a direct INSERT.

**Edge function**
- Amount is never read from the request body. For orders the server prices the
  cart; for top-ups the requested amount is range-checked (₹1–₹50,000).
- Settlement = signature check **+** `razorpay.payments.fetch()` (status must be
  `captured`; `authorized` is captured explicitly) **+** intent ownership check
  **+** idempotent RPC. Signature comparison is length-safe/constant-time.

**Flutter**
- `checkout.dart` sends `{address_id, cart_items:[{variant_id, quantity}]}` and
  opens Razorpay with the amount the server returned; verification posts only
  `{intent_id, order_id, payment_id, signature}`.
- `walletpage.dart` no longer posts an amount.
- `DeliveryChargeService` asks `quote_cart` first and keeps the local tier math
  as a display-only offline fallback.

## 3. Verification

- `docs/db/tests/verify_money_integrity.mjs` — runs a real Postgres in-process
  (PGlite), loads `schema.sql` + `functions.sql`, replicates Supabase's roles and
  default privileges, applies 007–012 and runs 37 attack/happy-path assertions.
  `cd docs/db/tests && npm install && npm run verify` → **37 passed, 0 failed**,
  including: tampered delivery charge (0 and negative) ignored, negative quantity
  rejected, cross-user order rejected, customer wallet-credit RPC rejected,
  `wallet_balance` not column-writable, subscription price taken from the
  catalog, buffer enforced, underpaid Razorpay settlement refused, payment replay
  idempotent, top-up credit capped, mismatched/duplicate Razorpay order ids
  refused, `finalize_daily_run` and ad-hoc claim spoofing blocked, the privilege
  surface sealed (`anon` limited to `quote_cart`/`has_permission`/
  `is_super_admin`), `update_address_as_default` ownership enforced and its
  column bug fixed, a staff direct-INSERT bypass aborted at COMMIT while an
  honest staff insert succeeds, `subscription_items.unit_price` snapped and
  immutable, daily-order total drift rejected, and the admin paths still working.
  Dropping any of 010–012 from the list makes the corresponding assertions fail.
- `npx tsc --noEmit` in `Hetha_admin` → clean (after the `dailyOps/orders.ts` fix).
- `flutter analyze` → **No issues found**.
- `deno check supabase/functions/razorpay/index.ts` → clean.

## 4. Deployment (app is pre-release — no installed builds to keep compatible)

1. `007_money_integrity.sql` — Supabase SQL editor, as the project owner.
2. `008_payment_intents.sql`
3. `009_privilege_lockdown.sql`
4. `010_grant_hardening.sql`
5. `011_legacy_function_grants.sql`
6. `012_money_invariants.sql` — before running it, use the two audit queries at
   the bottom of that file to list any pre-existing rows that already violate the
   invariants (a legacy `AH-…` order from the old direct-INSERT admin code is the
   likely hit). The triggers only fire on new/changed rows, so old rows are not
   rejected retroactively — but they will block a later edit to their amounts.
7. Deploy the edge function: `supabase functions deploy razorpay`
   (env `key_id` / `key_secret` unchanged), or paste
   `Hetha_app/supabase/functions/razorpay/index.ts` into the dashboard editor.
8. Rebuild the Flutter app from this working tree. The admin panel also needs a
   redeploy for the `dailyOps/orders.ts` fix.

Steps 7 and 8 are a breaking change to the payment contract: `create-order` now
requires `purpose`, and verification requires `intent_id`. Any build made before
this commit can no longer complete an online payment, which is fine pre-release —
just don't keep testing with an older APK.

Before/while testing, wipe the test-data damage the old holes may have left:
compare each `users.wallet_balance` against the sum of its `wallet_transactions`
(the fixes lock the balance down, they don't correct a balance that was already
inflated), and clear out any `orders` rows with a suspicious
`delivery_charge`/`total`.

Post-deploy checks: admin wallet adjust (`customers:edit`), admin ad-hoc order
and subscription creation, daily-ops finalize, customer wallet top-up, wallet
order, COD order, online order, subscription create/pause/cancel.

## 5. Residual risks (deliberately not changed)

- **Serviceability / cutoff times / delivery frequency** are still validated
  only in the client. A tampered client can order to an unserviced pincode or
  past the cutoff. Not a pricing issue, but the same class of trust.
- **COD** remains available to customers; an abusive customer can still generate
  orders they never pay for. Business control, not a code control.
- **Subscription status** is still customer-updatable (needed by the app's
  pause/cancel flows); only `user_id` and `payment_method` are protected.
- **`razorpay_payment_service.dart` hard-codes the test key id**
  (`rzp_test_…`) — fine (it's public), but switch it and the edge function's
  `key_id`/`key_secret` together when going live.
- **No Razorpay webhook.** If the app dies between capture and verification the
  payment succeeds with no order; the intent stays `created` and the money needs
  a manual refund/credit. A `payment.captured` webhook calling the same
  `finalize_*` RPCs would close this (they're already idempotent).
- Stale `payment_pending` orders and expired intents are not swept; add a cron
  job if they accumulate.
- The pre-existing latent bugs documented in `docs/db/functions.sql`
  (`update_address_as_default`, scheduled `cancel_subscription`, `get_user_role`)
  are untouched.
